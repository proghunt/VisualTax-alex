The Financial Planning Process consists of six basic steps.

1. Current position

2. Goals and Objectives

3. Analysis

4. Action Plan

5. Implementation

6. Review

The Visual FP program provides full implementation of all steps in an integrated way unique among financial planning software. 

1 Current position

This is the first and most time consuming step in the financial planning process and it should be prepared carefully as most of the remaining steps rely on its accuracy.

In this step information is gathered on the present financial position of your client.

Particular attention should be paid in the classification and recording of the Assets and the Liabilities. One should try to provide ALL the information for each Asset or Liability including Account numbers and Rate of Return where applicable.

The objective is to arrive at:

An accurate Net Worth Statement (Balance Sheet)

Current Income sources and Expenses and projected ones for Retirement or Disability. This helps determine any current or future cash flow problems and shortfalls.

Current tax situation (imported from the tax return)

2 Goals and Objectives

The client and the Financial Planner discuss and agree on the goals and objectives.

They can be expressed in general terms or expressed in very specific terms such as retirement at a specific age. The Retirement Income and Expenses objective has been determined during the data Gathering process under Annual Income Sources and Expenses.

Special attention should be paid to the specific objectives and Life Events which quantify special desires such as financing the children’s education, or financing an expensive trip. Any items that are expected to have an impact on the balance sheet such as major purchases or sales can also be recorded. The program will take them into account in the retirement projection calculations.

3 Analysis

Normally this step is done by the Financial Planner on his own by analyzing the Balance sheet, Income Expenses, client Objectives/Risk tolerance and the Disposable Income.

The problems are classified and expressed in general terms such as Cash Flow problem, Poor investments, inadequate insurance, etc.

Based on the Disposable Income calculation the Financial Planner will determine how much of the disposable Income should be used for debt reduction, insurance or investments.

The Life insurance worksheet will determine any additional insurance required expressed in $50,000 increments.

The amount used for investment purposes will have an impact on the retirement planning calculations and projections.

3.1 Retirement Analysis

Retirement projections are calculated by the program based on the specific retirement goals, the Life Events and the Investment Strategy chosen.

The Investment Strategy defines the Asset Allocation into different types of investments and the expected rate of return from each type of investment.

Based on the above the program calculates any Shortfall at retirement, and the year by year Asset Accumulation for the various investment types.

The calculation algorithm tries to meet the client objectives by utilizing the remaining balances in the investments following the sequence of:

Non-sheltered

Semi-sheltered

Sheltered

Analysis of the Asset Accumulation will indicate if the client can meet his/her future financial objectives. Negative balances in the non-sheltered investments indicates that the client cannot meet the objectives, even after the other investments have been liquidated. In this case the objectives or the investment strategy should be revised.

4 Action Plan

The Financial Planner in consultation with the Client arrive at the appropriate action plan. This may involve selling some assets or replacing certain investments with others that better fit the client profile and his/her objectives.

Responsibility for carrying out a specific action is assigned to client or the Financial Planner.

The program will automatically assign responsibility to the Financial Planner for all actions that require the handling of Mutual Funds.

The impact of the action plan is reflected on the automatically prepared Net Worth Statement and the various retirement projections.

Specific Buying, Redeeming or Switching Mutual Funds can be entered by the Financial Planner. These actions can eventually be converted automatically to Mutual Funds orders ready to be sent to a Mutual Fund Company or Mutual Fund dealer.

4.1 Portfolio Creator

The program provides facilities to build several investment portfolios for a given client, such as RRSP, or Non-RRSP. Each portfolio should be given a unique name to reflect its type and purpose and/or the date it was build. One of the portfolios should be given the status of ‘Active’. Obsolete portfolios should be deleted or classified as ‘Inactive’.

Once a portfolio has been created, the total amount of investment should be entered against it. The program provides facilities to select the funds comprising the portfolio and their percentage of the total investment portfolio amount. Historical performance of each fund can be viewed on the screen as well as projected performance for the total portfolio.

A portfolio, if agreed by the client can be converted to Mutual Funds orders.

5 Implementation and Transactions

In this phase of the process the Financial Planner or Agent acts on the various recommendations that came up during the Action Plan and/or the portfolio creator process.

Facilities provided by the program are geared specifically to handling Mutual Fund orders. The investment plan designed during the Action Plan and the Mutual Funds from the designed portfolios can be converted to Mutual Fund orders. Mutual Fund Agents will be transmitting the orders to their Mutual Funds Dealer who in turn will interact with the Mutual Funds Companies.

The Mutual Fund Dealer must have our Visual FP - Dealer Version of the program.

Mutual Fund transactions can be created on demand, without having to first create an Investment Plan or a Mutual Funds Portfolio.

6 Performance Review

The Financial Planner and Client should meet periodically to review the performance of the Financial Planning recommendations. Specifically, performance of investments should be reviewed at least every six months to determine deviations from the plan and arrive at a new course of action if necessary.

The Visual FP program provides facilities to update the financial position of the client based on latest prices of the various investments.
