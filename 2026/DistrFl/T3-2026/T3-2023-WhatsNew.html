FEDERAL

Schedule 15, FHSA Contributions, Transfers and Activities

First home savings account (FHSA)

Effective for the 2023 and subsequent tax years, Schedule 15 is used to calculate the annual FHSA limit, FHSA deduction and unused FHSA contributions available to deduct in future years, and to report qualifying withdrawals from the FHSAs. The following lines have been added on the Schedule 15:

68930 New FHSAs indicator

68935 Total contributions made to FHSAs in the tax year

68940 Total contributions made to FHSAs in the tax year after the first qualifying withdrawal

68945 Total amount of designated withdrawals from FHSAs made in the tax year

68950 Total transfers made from RRSPs to FHSAs in the tax year

68955 Total amount of designated transfers from FHSAs to RRSPs or RRIFs made in the year

68960 Total amount of qualifying withdrawals made in the tax year 68965 FHSA home address indicato

The first home savings account (FHSA) is a new registered plan to help individuals save for their first home. Starting April 1, 2023, contributions to an FHSA will be deductible and the income earned in an FHSA will not be taxable. Qualifying withdrawals made from an FHSA to purchase or build a first home are also non-taxable. Complete the new Schedule 15, FHSA Contributions, Transfers and Activities, if you opened an FHSA in 2023 or if you made a contribution and/or transfer from your RRSP to the FHSA. For more information about the FHSA, go to canada.ca/fhsa. See Schedule 15 for new lines added.

The following lines have been added on the tax return:

12905 First home savings account (FHSA) income

12906 FHSA income – other 20805 FHSA deduction

S12- Multigenerational home renovation tax credit

New for 2023 and subsequent tax year, the Schedule 12 will be used to capture the qualifying expenditures for the multigenerational home renovation tax credit. The following line has been added on the Schedule 12: 45354 Qualified expenditure

The multigenerational home renovation tax credit is a new refundable tax credit introduced for the 2023 and subsequent tax years. An individual may claim the expenditures on the renovation to a dwelling that is completed to allow a qualifying relation to live with them. The expenditures are claimed on the new Schedule 12 and the tax credit is claimed on page 8 of the tax return.

The following line has been added on the Schedule 12:

45354 Qualified expenditures The following line has been on the return:

45355 Multigenerational home renovation tax credit

Schedule 3, Capital Gains (or Losses)

Flipping Property

When a property is acquired with the intention of reselling it in a short period to realize a profit, the profit should be characterized as business income and not as a capital gain. A new question will be added on the Schedule 3, Capital Gains (or Losses), to capture if the taxpayer disposed of a residential property that was owned for less than 365 consecutive days, unless the disposition was in relation to at least one of nine life events as specified on the Schedule 3.

The following lines have been added on the Schedule 3:

17905 Disposition of property owned for less than 365 consecutive days

17906 Disposition due to, or in anticipation of, life events

Schedule 6, Canada Workers Benefit

Currently, a taxpayer may receive the Canada workers benefit (CWB) advance payments upon request. The payment is recorded on the RC210 slip and is reported on line 41500 of the tax return. For couples, the individual who received the CWB advance payments must claim the CWB. For 2023 and subsequent tax years, the process of submitting an application to request CWB advance payments will be eliminated. The new advanced Canada workers benefit, based on the prior year return, will be issued automatically as a benefit during the tax year. The amount on line 41500 will depend on the amounts calculated for line 45300 and on the RC210 slip. It is calculated as the lesser of the CWB and the advanced CWB for the year. Moreover, if the individual and their spouse or common-law partner are both eligible, they can now choose who will claim the basic CWB and the individual claiming CWB will report the basic advanced CWB received for the family. For the disability supplement, spouses will report and claim the advanced CWB and CWB individually.

The following lines have been added on the Schedule 6:

38120 Total basic advanced Canada workers benefit paid to the individual

38121 Total basic advanced Canada workers benefit paid to individual’s spouse or common-law partner

38122 Total advanced Canada workers benefit disability supplement paid to the individual The following line has been revised on the tax return:

41500 Advanced Canada workers benefi

PROVINCIAL TAXES AND CREDITS

Newfoundland and Labrador

Physical activity tax credit For tax years 2021 and 2022, individuals may claim 8.7% of up to $2,000 of eligible physical activity expenses. Effective for 2023 and subsequent years, the rate has been doubled, from 8.7% to 17.4%, of up to $2,000 of such eligible expenses.

The following line on the Form NL479 remains unchanged:

62000 Physical activity expense

Ontario

Staycation tax credit Jobs training tax credit Seniors’ home safety tax credit Effective for tax year 2023, the above refundable tax credits have been eliminated. In addition, the Schedule ON(S12) is no longer valid. The following lines have been removed from the Form ON479: 63051 Staycation indicator 63052 Staycation expenses 63055 Job training tax credit 63105 Seniors’ home safety tax credit The following line on the Schedule ON(S12) is no longer valid: 60890 Involuntary separation indicator

Saskatchewan

Home renovation expenses Effective for 2023 and subsequent tax years, the above non-refundable tax credit has been eliminated.

The following line on the forms SK428 and T2203 has been removed: 58340 Home renovation expenses

Mineral exploration tax credit For 2023 and subsequent tax years, there is no calculation change to the above tax credit. However, lines 63600, 63601, 63602 and 63603 will be moved from the Form SK428 to the new Form T1279, Saskatchewan Mineral Exploration Tax Credit

The following lines have been moved from the Form SK428 to the new Form T1279, and removed from the Form SK428MJ: 63600 Mineral exploration tax credit 63601 Amount to carry back to the first prior year 63602 Amount to carry back to the second prior year 63603 Amount to carry back to the third prior year

British Columbia

Clean buildings tax credit Clean buildings tax credit allocated from a partnership Effective for tax years 2023 to 2027, these tax credits are introduced to encourage retrofitting of existing buildings to reduce energy use. Calculate the tax credits on Form T1356, British Columbia Clean Buildings Tax Credit, and claim the amounts on Form BC479.

The following lines have been added to the Form BC479:

60545 Clean buildings tax credit

60546 Clean buildings tax credit allocated from partnerships Renter’s tax credit

Effective for tax year 2023 and subsequent, eligible individuals can claim this refundable tax credit by completing Form BC479. The following lines have been added to the Form BC479:

60575 Eligible rental months

60576 Renter’s tax credit

Yukon

Mining business carbon price rebate Effective for tax year 2023 and subsequent, a new rebate for mining businesses is available in addition to the existing business carbon price rebate. Both the existing business rebate and the new mining business rebate will be calculated on the Schedule YT(S14) and the rebate amounts will be claimed on the Form YT479.

The following line has been added to the Form YT479:

63856 Mining business carbon price rebate The following line on the Form YT479 has been renamed:

63855 General business carbon price rebate
