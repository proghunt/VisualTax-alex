HIGHLIGHTS OF CHANGES MADE TO THE VISUALTAX PROGRAM:

We introduced the Enterprise Version this year for several 2024-2025 products and it is now our top of the line version.

A brief introduction of what the Enterprise includes over and above the Power version is spelled out on the 2024 Order form. You may find more info about those items such as Special Election Returns (SERS), Foreign Reporting (FRRMS) and Capital Gains taxation changes in the CRA's literature. Such items are going to be introduced within the next few weeks and months.

The Enterprise versions are priced a bit higher than the Power versions as shown on the Order form, but they include additional forms, new types of tax returns, more automatic calculations, and more features and diagnostics over and above the Power versions. The price of the Power versions in general remained the same as last year and for a lot of professional tax preparers may still be a good alternative.  Rollover from any version used last year will work into the Enterprise version of this year. 

Upgrade to the Enterprise versions is voluntary. The enterprise versions will cover the so many new multi-page forms and return types that CRA is relentlessly introducing each year or require a high cost of developing and certifying them with CRA. 

We would recommend them for mature offices that need to fly first class and have their best chances to serve the needs of any new client who walks into their office. Upgrades to the Enterprise version will also be available after February, but the price is not known at this time.


HIGHLIGHTS OF THE MOST IMPORTANT CRA CHANGES KNOWN AT THIS TIME

Some changes listed below are in a WIP and currently they undergoing testing.

2024 Income Tax and Benefit Return

Capital gains

Under proposed changes, the capital gains inclusion rate will increase to 2/3 for capital gains realized after June 24, 2024. However, a new capital gains reduction will apply so that the effective inclusion rate for capital gains of $250,000 or less will remain at 1/2. In addition, the lifetime capital gains exemption will increase for dispositions occurring after June 24, 2024. A new capital gains reduction add-back, deduction for qualifying business transfers, and additional security options deduction will also be introduced. For more information about these changes, go to canada.ca/taxes-capital-gains or see Guide T4037, Capital Gains.

This is by far the biggest change for the tax software. It starts with S3, breakind down the dispositions in two periods as each one uses a different rate and treatment and Efiling. This change has a profound affect in so may other forms and calculations such as T657, T1A, ABIL, T1170, T691, T1212, T2091, T1 Jacket, etc..

Canada pension plan (CPP) and Quebec pension plan (QPP) enhancement

A second CPP/QPP contribution limit was introduced January 1, 2024. This new limit, or second earnings ceiling, subjects worker’s earnings to two earnings limits. Employees and employers each contribute an additional 4% on earnings above the first earnings ceiling, up to the amount of the second earnings ceiling. For self-employment income and other earnings, an additional 8% is contributed by the individual. Contributions through employment will appear in box 16A and 17A of the T4 slip as “Employee’s second CPP contributions (CPP2)” and “Employee’s second QPP contributions (QPP2), respectively. For more information about the CPP enhancement, go to canada.ca/cpp-enhancement. For information about the QPP enhancement and QPP contributions, go to canada.ca/qpp-contributions-paid and select “Québec Pension Plan Contributions.” As of January 1, 2024, employees who are aged 73 or older are no longer required to contribute into the QPP. Likewise, their employers are not required to withhold QPP.

This is the second big change. So big that it forced the RC381 form to baloon to 18 pages of heavy calculation details, as well as S8 to reach 10 pages of heavy calculations.

Short-term rentals (T776)

Tax deductions for expenses incurred relating to non-compliant short-term rentals will be denied. This change applies to all expenses, including interest expenses, incurred to earn income (outlays and expenses incurred after 2023) by operating short-term rental properties. For more information about these changes, see Guide T4036, Rental Income.

EFILE for non-residents

Starting with the 2023 tax year, the EFILE service began accepting and processing the following non-resident types:

 • Emigrant taxpayers

 • Section 116 returns

 Starting with the 2024 tax year, the EFILE service will begin to also accept and process returns for deemed residents of Canada.

Canada Carbon Rebate

Starting from the 2024 tax year, the Climate Action Incentive Payment (CAIP) is renamed to the Canada Carbon Rebate (CCR).

Home Buyers’ Plan withdrawals

The Home Buyer’s Plan withdrawal limit has increased to $60,000 for withdrawals made after April 16, 2024. In addition, the 15-year repayment period now starts in the fifth year after the first withdrawal is made.

Alternative minimum tax

Several changes have been introduced primarily caused by the Capital Gains changes.

Consent to share contact information – Organ and tissue donor registry

Bill C-210 authorizes the CRA to enter into an agreement with a provincial or territorial government to collect any information using the tax return filed by a taxpayer, that the province or territory requires for the purpose of establishing or maintaining an organ and tissue donor registry in the province or territory. As of the 2024 tax year, an agreement has been reached between the CRA and British Columbia.

PROVINCIAL TAXES AND CREDITS

PE children’s wellness tax credit For tax year 2024 and subsequent, the maximum amount for the children’s wellness tax credit has been increased from $500 to $1,000.

NB volunteer firefighters’ amount and search and rescue volunteers' amount: For tax year 2024 and subsequent, these new non-refundable tax credits are available to eligible residents of New Brunswick. The maximum amount for either credit is $5,000.

Ontario tax reduction Low-income individuals and families tax (LIFT) credit If the taxpayer's spouse or common-law partner died at any time during the year, then neither the taxpayer nor their spouse or common-law partner is considered to have a 'cohabiting spouse or common-law partner at the end of the year'. Consequently, for tax year 2024 and subsequent, the deceased individual or their surviving spouse or common-law partner will: • claim the Ontario tax reduction as if they were a single individual not include their spouse's or common-law partner's income when calculating their adjusted family net income for the purpose of their respective Ontario LIFT credit. Ontario health premiums Currently, the Ontario health premium (OHP) is calculated based on the taxable income reported by the taxpayer at line 26000 of their return. For tax year 2024 and subsequent, the OHP will be calculated based on the sum of the following: the taxpayer's taxable income reported at line 26000, and the taxpayer's split income deduction claimed at line 23200 (line 68360 of Form T1206, Tax on Split Income), if any.

Manitoba fertility treatment tax credit: For tax year 2024 and subsequent, the maximum eligible medical expenses for fertility treatments have been increased from $20,000 to $40,000.
